🔬 Adobe Research Scientist Intern

Summer 2025 📍 Seattle, WA

An incredible foundational experience! I worked as a Research Scientist/Engineer Intern in Seattle, WA with my amazing mentor Jane Hoffswell, developing a multi-agent LLM framework to help practitioners create effective visualizations.

This experience is a big part of why I'm now interested in leveraging multi-agent systems for user tasks and helping users perceive data or enhance storytelling through data visualization.

Community Intern Captain Highlight: One of my proudest achievements was serving as the Community Intern Captain alongside two other incredible interns for the Seattle Office. We organized multiple outdoor events including:

  • ⚾ Baseball game experiences
  • 🏔️ Hiking adventures at Mount Rainier
  • 🏞️ Picnic at the Park gatherings

This leadership role helped me connect with people from different research areas, discover synergies, and brainstorm how we can leverage vision and AI tasks in the context of visualization.

How I got it: I reached out to Jane in early November when I was working on my thesis and brainstorming project ideas. During our conversation, I pitched my research interests and discovered they were exploring something similar — a good reminder that a clear, well-targeted cold email can lead to great opportunities. 🚀

👨‍🏫 Teaching Experience — 6.67/7

Fall 2024 📍 Introduction to Visualization

Grateful to my advisor Dr. Alvitta Ottley for this experience. I scored 6.67/7 (Department Average: 6.11/7, School Average: 6.18/7) for co-instructing "Introduction to Visualization."

My teaching journey started during undergrad, TAing Computer Science, Physics, and Mathematics courses. I genuinely enjoy helping students — often extending sessions when needed and learning alongside them.

I taught data visualization including data wrangling, D3.js, design guidelines, what makes good vs. bad visualizations, perception, and prototyping, and helped students build final dashboards for social good.

The best moment was when a student approached me at a campus cafe to say the course was their favorite, and that their final project even helped them land an internship. ✨

📊 American Institutes for Research (AIR) Doctoral Student Intern

Summer 2024 📍 Remote / Washington, DC

A transformative research experience. Though the internship was remote, I spent a week in Washington, DC for orientation. I worked with mentors Brittany Boyd and Cadelle Hemphill on interactive tools to help Grade-8 teachers investigate student performance on Math scores.

Primary Project: I performed comprehensive statistical analysis across factors including race, internet access, and access to technology at school and home. This work connects directly to my research on how different populations interact with and interpret data visualizations.

Secondary Environmental Project: I also worked with Rachel Carroll, who became a great mentor (she even extended my internship). We investigated environmental factors on student performance using ML and regression models to predict air pollution across North Carolina. I got to work with an amazing group of folks at AIR on this — Rachel Carroll, Paul Bailey, Dana McCalla, and Thomas Snyder.

Technical Contributions:

  • 🔍 Pulled data from PurpleAir across locations in North Carolina
  • 📊 Worked with EPA data for environmental analysis
  • 🗺️ Explored kriging and clustering methods to predict pollutant levels
  • 📈 Investigated relationships between environmental factors and student performance

This work was published as "Estimating PM2.5 Concentrations at Public Schools in North Carolina Using Multiple Data Sources and Interpolation Methods" in Nature Scientific Reports. 🚀

🔬 How I Got Into Visualization Research

August 2021 📍 WashU Beginning

The beginning of my PhD journey. In my final year at Beloit College, I had both industry offers and graduate school acceptances, but I wanted a PhD regardless. As a first-generation college student, education was always a priority in my family.

I chose WashU. I'd always been intrigued by visualizations in sports (cricket, badminton, football) and noticed how poorly designed visualizations proliferate in news and social media — my dad would share charts without noticing something was off. That pushed me toward a PhD in visualization / HCI.

Finding My Mentor: Prof. Alvitta Ottley was working on information visualization and communicating data to different stakeholders. While rotating with her, she gave me a project on measuring whether people can actually read data visualizations — exactly my interest.

The Mini-VLAT Project: I took it on without fully knowing what it would involve — literature review, user studies, iterating on experimental issues, writing it up, and eventually earning a first-author Best Paper Award at EuroVIS. 🏆

Germany & Conference Glory: Traveling to Germany to present, and being the first to present in front of the whole conference (best paper talks get the main stage instead of a small session), was incredible — and the real start of my PhD journey.
