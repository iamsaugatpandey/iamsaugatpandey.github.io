  1. Nov 2026
    Greenville / Spartanburg, SC
    Media

    On WSPA 7News: AI agents and privacy

    Talking AI on local TV. WSPA 7News interviewed me for their consumer segment “AI agent at your service: Is this new tech right for you?” We talked about the new wave of consumer AI agents that act on your behalf, and what to think about before you hand one access to your accounts and personal data.

    My advice: limit how much independence you give these agents, take the companies' own warnings that they make mistakes seriously, and watch out for prompt injection, where hidden instructions can hijack an agent into doing things you never asked for.

    Read the story on WSPA · Watch the segment on YouTube

  2. Summer 2025
    Seattle, WA
    Internship

    Research Scientist Intern, Adobe

    An incredible foundational experience. I worked as a Research Scientist/Engineer Intern in Seattle with my amazing mentor Jane Hoffswell, developing a multi-agent LLM framework to help practitioners create effective visualizations.

    Read moreShow less

    This experience is a big part of why I'm now interested in leveraging multi-agent systems for user tasks and helping people perceive data or tell stories through data visualization.

    Community Intern Captain. One of my proudest achievements was serving as Community Intern Captain for the Seattle office alongside two other incredible interns. We organized outdoor events including:

    • Baseball game outings
    • A hike at Mount Rainier
    • Picnics in the park

    That role helped me connect with people from different research areas, find synergies, and brainstorm how vision and AI tasks fit into visualization.

    How I got it. I reached out to Jane in early November while I was working on my thesis and brainstorming project ideas. I pitched my research interests and discovered they were exploring something similar: a good reminder that a clear, well-targeted cold email can lead to great opportunities.

  5. Fall 2024
    Washington University in St. Louis
    Teaching

    Teaching Introduction to Visualization: 6.67 out of 7

    Grateful to my advisor Dr. Alvitta Ottley for this experience. I was rated 6.67/7 for co-instructing “Introduction to Visualization” (department average 6.11, school average 6.18).

    My teaching started in undergrad, TAing Computer Science, Physics, and Mathematics courses. I genuinely enjoy helping students, often extending sessions when needed and learning alongside them.

    I taught data wrangling, D3.js, design guidelines, what makes good and bad visualizations, perception, and prototyping, and helped students build final dashboards for social good.

    The best moment was when a student stopped me at a campus cafe to say the course was their favorite, and that their final project helped them land an internship.

  6. Summer 2024
    Remote / Washington, DC
    Internship

    Doctoral Student Intern, American Institutes for Research

    A transformative research experience. The internship was remote, but I spent a week in Washington, DC for orientation. I worked with mentors Brittany Boyd and Cadelle Hemphill on interactive tools to help Grade 8 teachers investigate student performance in math.

    Read moreShow less

    Primary project. I ran statistical analyses across factors including race, internet access, and access to technology at school and home. This connects directly to my research on how different populations interact with and interpret data visualizations.

    Environmental project. I also worked with Rachel Carroll, who became a great mentor (she even extended my internship). We studied environmental factors in student performance, using ML and regression models to predict air pollution across North Carolina, with an amazing group at AIR: Rachel Carroll, Paul Bailey, Dana McCalla, and Thomas Snyder.

    • Pulled PurpleAir sensor data from across North Carolina
    • Worked with EPA data for environmental analysis
    • Explored kriging and clustering methods to predict pollutant levels
    • Investigated relationships between environmental factors and student performance

    This work was published as “Estimating PM2.5 Concentrations at Public Schools in North Carolina Using Multiple Data Sources and Interpolation Methods” in Scientific Reports.

  7. Aug 2021
    WashU, the beginning
    PhD

    How I got into visualization research

    The beginning of my PhD journey. In my final year at Beloit College I had both industry offers and graduate school acceptances, but I wanted a PhD regardless. As a first-generation college student, education was always a priority in my family.

    Read moreShow less

    I chose WashU. I'd always been intrigued by visualizations in sports (cricket, badminton, football) and noticed how poorly designed charts spread through news and social media. My dad would share charts without noticing something was off. That pushed me toward a PhD in visualization and HCI.

    Finding my mentor. Prof. Alvitta Ottley was working on information visualization and communicating data to different stakeholders. While I was rotating with her, she gave me a project on measuring whether people can actually read data visualizations, which was exactly my interest.

    The Mini-VLAT project. I took it on without fully knowing what it would involve: literature review, user studies, iterating on experimental issues, writing it up, and eventually a first-author Best Paper Award at EuroVis.

    Presenting in Germany. Traveling to Germany to present, and being the first talk in front of the whole conference (best-paper talks get the main stage), was incredible, and the real start of my PhD.
